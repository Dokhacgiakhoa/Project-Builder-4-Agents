# Product Thinking — Core Concepts

## Module 1: Problem First
- Common pitfall: Jumping straight into solution brainstorming.
- A viable feature = A sellable capability = Solves real pain + Target audience is willing to pay. That is an **Opportunity**.
- A Product Manager must answer 2 fundamental questions: **Who** is the focus (user segment sharing an unmet need)? **What** problem is being solved (is the pain acute enough to warrant payment/adoption)?
- "Fall in love with the problem, not the solution."
- Analytical framework: **Who → What → How**.

## Module 2: Outcome over Output
- Many products achieve millions of users without generating sustainable revenue.
- Exceptional PMs drive sustainable enterprise growth, not merely customer delight or prolific feature delivery.
- Pure unconstrained customer-centricity can harm the business if unit economics and enterprise goals are ignored.
- Start from key metrics: Which metric must grow → Which opportunity should be pursued?
- Opportunity = A viable customer cohort with unmet needs, willing to "pay" (with currency, effort, or time).
- High-leverage features steer user behavior to drive measurable outcomes.
- 3 types of outcomes:
  - **Business Outcome**: Revenue, gross margins, market share, retention, LTV/CAC, free-to-paid conversion.
  - **Desired Customer Outcome**: Efficiency (faster, cheaper, higher volume) or transformation (delightful, accessible, novel capabilities).
  - **Product Outcome** (Intersection of desired + business): Feature adoption, task completion rates, retention curves, engagement depth.

## Module 3: 2 Visual Mapping Frameworks
- **Impact Mapping** (Strategic level): Business Goal → Actors → Desired Impacts (Behavioral changes) → Deliverables.
- **Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)**: Desired Outcome → Opportunities (Unmet customer pains) → Potential Solutions → Assumptions & Experiments.
  - Example: Increase onboarding completion from 40% to 65% in Q2 → Users fail to see value within first 5 minutes → Personalized AI welcome flow → A/B test on 10% of new signups, measuring time-to-first-value.

## Case Study: MoMo Piggy Bank (Heo Dat MoMo)
- **Who**: Existing consumer base of MoMo e-wallet, mainstream users with modest donation budgets.
- **What**: Desire to contribute to social causes but reluctant to make negligible micro-donations; enjoys casual gamification.
- **How**: Virtual piggy bank matures through daily transactions and interactive check-ins. When the piggy bank matures, corporate sponsors convert virtual points into real philanthropic capital.
- **Desired Outcome**: Fun, social pride, frictionless charitable impact at zero personal cost.
- **Business Outcome**: Increased MAU, increased donation volume (CSR branding), higher overall transaction frequency.
- **Product Outcome**: Onboarding completion rates, monthly active days, user retention, weekly mission completions.

## Summary Checklist
Customer-Centric Framing + Outcome over Output + Impact Mapping + Opportunity Solution Tree (OST).
