### 2. The 5-minute script (Demo Day / Final Defense)

* **Minute 0 - 1: Context, a shocking number & the target customer**:
  - State the audience clearly: CTOs, Tech Leads and software engineers struggling with the technical debt (Technical Debt) AI creates.
  - Quote a real pilot customer.
* **Minute 1 - 2.5: Live Demo Showcase**:
  - Open the product screen or the workflow diagram live:
    1. *Step 1*: Issue the Spec & freeze the API Contract.
    2. *Step 2*: The Agent reads the context, breaks it into subtasks by itself and writes tests following TDD.
    3. *Step 3*: Switch between interface styles (UI/UX Presets) back and forth with just 1 command.
    4. *Step 4*: The CI Pipeline confirms Anti-Crash automatically before allowing a merge.
* **Minute 2.5 - 3.5: Revenue model, Traction & Competitive Moat**:
  - A comparison table with competitors (traditional Starter kits vs No-code black-box).
  - Growth rate: the number of stars, forks, or users who signed up to try it in the last 4 weeks.
* **Minute 3.5 - 4.5: Team, Use of funds & 18-month Milestones**:
  - Briefly explain why this team has an "Unfair Advantage".
  - A transparent budget allocation (65% R&D, 20% GTM, 15% Ops).
* **Minute 4.5 - 5.0: Conclusion & Call to Action**.

## 🛡️ PART 2: THE 20 "KILLER" CHALLENGE QUESTIONS FROM JUDGES / VCS & HOW TO ANSWER

### Group 1: Moat & Defensibility

#### 1. "If OpenAI, Anthropic or Microsoft GitHub ship a similar feature tomorrow, does your project die?"
* **Answer strategy (The Platform-Agnostic Defense)**:
  > *"That is an excellent question. OpenAI or Anthropic build the engine (Foundation Models), while we build the 'chassis and steering wheel' (Governance & Architecture Framework). They make money by selling tokens, so they want users to generate as much code as possible. By contrast, we protect the integrity of the enterprise system by controlling context and stopping junk code. Moreover, our product is fully Model-Agnostic — today the customer uses Claude, tomorrow they can switch to GPT-5 or Gemini without depending on a single provider."*

#### 2. "This is just a repository template on GitHub; if a competitor forks your source code, do you lose everything?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"An open-source template is the funnel that attracts users (Acquisition Funnel), not the whole commercial value. The value that retains customers (Moat) sits in 3 layers: (1) a community of contributors and a Best Practices system updated every week; (2) the proprietary Cloud Evaluator and CI Quality Gate for enterprises; (3) the Switching Cost once a team's whole collaboration process is bound to this standard."*

#### 3. "Why don't large companies build their own internal starter kit?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"Many companies tried, but the cost of keeping an internal team to research, maintain and continuously track the dizzying changes of AI SDKs and UI libraries is at least 200,000 USD a year. They are willing to pay a small SaaS fee of $49-$299/month to use a common standard already validated by [number, with a source] engineers (state this only when you have real data)."*

### Group 2: Tech, Cost & Scalability

#### 4. "Token API costs for automation Agents are expensive; is your profit margin negative?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"We designed a layered Context architecture (Context Pruning & Spec-Driven). Instead of throwing a million lines of code into the prompt, which is costly and causes hallucination, the Agent reads only a short Spec file (under 2,000 tokens) and executes within exactly that module's scope. This aims to cut token cost compared with ordinary vibe coding (the reduction of [X%] must be measured by a benchmark), with a gross margin target of [X%] (a real financial model is needed)."*

#### 5. "What prevents AI from hallucinating code or creating serious security holes?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"We apply the **'Human-in-the-loop & Approval Lock'** principle. AI never has the right to merge directly into the `main` branch. All AI-generated code must pass automated TDD tests, a strict security linter, and finally carry the approval (Approve) signature of a Human Tech Lead checked against the Definition of Done."*

#### 6. "Why did you choose Clean Architecture and Bulletproof React instead of a complex Microservices architecture?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"For [X%] of startup projects (a source is needed) in the 0-1 stage, Microservices is an 'overload trap' that kills the team with infrastructure cost and network communication latency. We chose a Modular Monolith with Clean Architecture and Feature-Driven React because it gives absolutely clear module boundaries, making the context easy for AI to understand and easy to debug, and when the scale reaches millions of users, splitting a feature module into an independent microservice takes [estimated time, needs measuring]."*

### Group 3: Market & Revenue Model

#### 7. "Is $49/month for an engineering team too expensive or too cheap?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"One working hour of a Senior Engineer in the US costs $80 - $120. If our product only helps that engineer save **a single hour per month** (avoiding the fix of one silly AI-generated bug), the customer already has a positive ROI. So the $49 price is a very attractive entry point for them to decide to pay without going through a complicated finance board."*

#### 8. "Isn't the 28-billion-USD TAM figure something you drew too large yourself?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"The 28-billion-USD figure is the size of the whole Developer Tools industry according to Gartner's report. However, we are not unrealistically targeting the whole TAM. Our real focus is the SOM segment worth $12 million in the next 18 months, calculated concretely from the bottom up (Bottom-up) with a goal of reaching 1,500 small and medium businesses ready to pay $8,000/year."*

#### 9. "What is your Go-To-Market strategy for the first 6 months without a huge advertising budget?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"We apply the Developer-First Content & Open Source PLG strategy thoroughly. We create deep technical teardowns of vibe coding disasters and release free tools such as the Style Presets Studio and the Marp Pitch Deck. Engineers hate sales ads but enthusiastically share open-source tools that help them solve real problems."*

#### 10. "Why wouldn't customers just use the free version forever instead of upgrading to pay?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"The free version is perfect for learning and personal projects. But when working inside an enterprise, customers must have: (1) role-based access control (RBAC); (2) Security & Compliance Reports; (3) a dashboard measuring the performance and safety of the Agents for the whole organization. These features exist only in the paid version."*

### Group 5: Trick Questions & Psychological Techniques

#### 13. "What is the biggest weakness of your product right now?"
* **Answer strategy (honest, with a solution)**:
  > *"Our current weakness is that it asks early users for a certain discipline of writing Specs and reading ADRs instead of just jumping in to type whimsical prompts. Our solution is to build an 'Interactive Interviewer' feature — the AI plays an interviewer who asks questions and generates the Spec for the user after only 5 minutes of conversation."*

#### 14. "If after 18 months you do not reach the revenue goal as promised, what will you do?"
* **Answer strategy**:
  > *"With a lean financial plan (Default Alive), we always keep spending at a minimum (Low burn-rate). If the market moves more slowly than expected, we have enough financial runway to adjust the product (Pivot) toward AI standardization training and consulting for enterprises — a business line with immediately positive cash flow."*
